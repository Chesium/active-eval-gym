\begin{abstract}%
Controllers learned in simulation can perform unevenly across plant parameters
and initial conditions. We propose to adapt training distributions using estimates
of where a controller meets a performance requirement. Each round evaluates a
frozen controller, targets training near its performance boundary, and evaluates
the update on fresh data. Linear systems and statistical certification provide
the course foundation. CartPole experiments and analysis of improvement and
regression form the main research, supporting an ambitious goal: conditions for
preserving or expanding acceptable regions under restricted controller updates.%
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

Learning from simulation helps control nonlinear systems when direct design is
difficult. Domain randomization trains across varying conditions, but its
distribution affects where a controller performs well. We investigate whether
evaluation can guide this distribution to improve coverage of a specified
operating domain, connecting data selection for controller learning to closed-loop
robustness analysis.

\section{Problem Formulation}

The proposed method connects three feedback loops:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Control:} the policy observes the system and applies actions,
      producing a closed-loop trajectory in each environment.
\item \textbf{Evaluation:} adaptively selected rollouts of a frozen policy
      estimate its performance level set and inform training-condition selection.
\item \textbf{Improvement:} the estimate guides the next training distribution;
      the updated policy is frozen and evaluated again.
\end{itemize}

At outer round $k$, freeze policy $\pi_k$; $t=0,\ldots,H-1$ indexes rollout
time. A condition $z=(\theta,s)\in\Zset$ specifies plant parameters $\theta$
and initial-state distribution $x_0\sim\nu_s$; remaining randomness has a
declared conditional law. For failure tolerance $\alpha$, let $p_k(z)$ be the
task-failure probability and $S_k=\{z:p_k(z)\le\alpha\}$ the acceptable region.
Under a fixed reference
probability measure $\mu$, coverage is $V_k=\mu(S_k)$. Gains
$g_k=\mu(S_{k+1}\setminus S_k)$ and losses $\ell_k=\mu(S_k\setminus S_{k+1})$
satisfy $V_{k+1}-V_k=g_k-\ell_k$. Training distribution $q_k$ adapts to
evaluation evidence; $\mu$ stays fixed. Certificates require direct rollout
evidence and apply only to the declared evaluation grid.

\textbf{RQ1 (empirical).} Does targeting training near the estimated reliability
boundary improve acceptable coverage compared with uniform and
intermediate-difficulty sampling, under matched budgets?
\textbf{RQ2 (theoretical).} Which assumptions on a parameterized system family,
controller updates, and performance margins bound regression or permit
acceptable-region expansion?

\textbf{Test problems.} A small linear family
$x_{t+1}=A(\theta)x_t+B(\theta)u_t$, with $u_t=-Kx_t$, supports interpretable
analysis. CartPole tests nonlinear policy learning over initial angle and pole
half-length. With its angle termination cutoff relaxed to $90^\circ$, recovery
requires survival for $500$ steps and final-100-step RMS angle at most $5^\circ$.
Other termination rules are retained. Recovery certificates concern finite-horizon
performance, not asymptotic stability.

\section{Goals and Approach}

\textbf{Stage 1: Course foundation.}
We will learn a linear controller under a fixed domain-randomization distribution
and derive a finite-horizon quadratic-cost sensitivity bound. Writing
$J_{H,K}(\theta,x)=x^\top P_H(K,\theta)x$, for $\|x\|_2\le r$ an update satisfies
$|J_{H,K'}-J_{H,K}|\le r^2\|P_H(K',\theta)-P_H(K,\theta)\|_2=:b(\theta)$.
Thus $J_{H,K}\le h-b(\theta)$ ensures preservation below cost threshold $h$.
In parallel, we will implement
level-set estimation and time-uniform confidence bounds: certify a grid condition
acceptable when its failure-probability upper bound is at most $\alpha$.
Fresh conditionally independent episodes and error allocation across conditions,
methods, and rounds yield simultaneous validity with error budget $\delta$.
Certified acceptable coverage at a fixed rollout budget assesses this evaluator.

\textbf{Stage 2: Main research.}
We will use the resulting evaluation evidence to adapt
$q_k=(1-\lambda)q_{\mathrm{base}}+\lambda q_{\mathrm{target},k}$.
Matched PPO branches from a common checkpoint compare uniform targets,
conditions near $\widehat p_k=\alpha$, and intermediate difficulty scored by
$\widehat p_k(1-\widehat p_k)$. The latter favors mixed outcomes rather than threshold proximity. We hold the
background mixture, training objective, optimizer, and budgets fixed and collect
fresh on-policy training trajectories. Fresh reference evaluations assess gains,
losses, and net coverage with uncertainty, seed variation, and total cost,
separating performance improvement from certificate growth. The linear family
supports analysis of these tradeoffs, including counterexamples to unconditional
improvement.

\textbf{Stage 3: Ambitious theoretical outcome.}
Building on the sensitivity result, we will seek restricted updates whose
performance drift and margins ensure preservation or net expansion.
Both cost and failure-probability level sets remain candidate targets;
transferring cost bounds to probabilities requires additional assumptions.
Whether repeated rounds are required remains open. The foundation and main
study remain deliverables without an expansion theorem.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Control and synthesis.} \citet{fujinami2025domain} analyze policy gradient
for domain-randomized LQR under bounded heterogeneity; \citet{fujinami2026history}
extend this to history-dependent controllers with a memory-expansion curriculum.
Their average-cost analysis motivates our linear study.
\textbf{Evaluation.} \citet{gotovos2013active} study active level-set estimation;
\citet{letham2022lookahead} address Bernoulli observations; and
\citet{howard2021timeuniform} develop confidence sequences.
\textbf{Improvement.} Reverse curricula \citep{florensa2017reverse}, prioritized
level replay \citep{jiang2021prioritized}, and sampling for learnability
\citep{rutherford2024noregrets} motivate our training-condition comparisons.
Lyapunov-based safe-region expansion
\citep{berkenkamp2017safe} concerns stability, distinct from our recovery criterion.
